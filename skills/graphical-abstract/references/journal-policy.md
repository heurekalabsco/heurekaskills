# Journal policy on AI-assisted imagery

Publisher policy on generated and AI-assisted figures is genuinely unsettled
and moves faster than any document can track. **This file deliberately names no
publisher's position.** Anything asserted here would be wrong within a year,
and a confidently stale policy claim is worse than none — it produces a figure
the author cannot submit, or a missing declaration they did not know they owed.

Check the live policy every time. The procedure below is stable; the answers
are not.

## Finding the policy

Search the publisher's own author guidance, not a summary of it:

- `<publisher> author guidelines artificial intelligence`
- `<journal> generative AI policy figures`
- `<publisher> AI-generated images editorial policy`

The relevant pages are usually titled "Editorial policies", "Author
responsibilities", "Artwork guidelines", or "AI use". Where a publisher has a
general AI policy and a separate artwork policy, read both — text and image
rules often differ, and the image rule is usually the stricter one.

Also check, in this order:

1. **The journal's own page**, which can be stricter than the publisher's
   house policy.
2. **The publisher's house policy**, which applies where the journal is silent.
3. **The relevant umbrella recommendations** for the field. In biomedical
   publishing, the ICMJE Recommendations are the common reference point that
   many journals adopt by citation; check the current text at `icmje.org`
   rather than assuming its contents.

## What you are looking for

Policies vary along four axes, and a policy can be permissive on one and strict
on another. Record all four:

| Axis | Question |
|---|---|
| Scope | Does the rule cover generated *artwork*, AI-*assisted* layout of author-supplied elements, or both? |
| Permission | Prohibited outright, permitted with disclosure, or unaddressed? |
| Disclosure location | Methods, acknowledgements, a cover letter, a submission-system field, or a figure legend? |
| Detail required | Tool name only, or tool plus version plus what it was used for? |

The distinction on the first axis matters most and is the one most often missed.
A policy prohibiting "AI-generated images" is usually aimed at photorealistic or
data-representing imagery. Whether it also covers a vector schematic drawn from
author-supplied content is frequently unclear — which is exactly the case to
flag to the author rather than resolve on their behalf.

## Recording the finding

Report the outcome in the figure's rationale, in this shape, so the author can
act on it without repeating the search:

```text
Journal:      <journal name>
Policy page:  <URL>
Checked:      <date>
Scope:        <generated artwork | AI-assisted layout | both | unclear>
Permission:   <prohibited | permitted with disclosure | not addressed>
Disclosure:   <where it goes, and what detail is required>
Action taken: <emitted figure | emitted specification only | flagged for author>
```

If the policy is unclear, say "unclear" and quote the sentence that is unclear.
Do not resolve ambiguity in the permissive direction.

## Declaration template

Adapt to the publisher's required wording and location. Where the publisher
supplies its own form of words, use theirs instead of this.

> **Declaration of AI-assisted figure preparation.** The graphical abstract in
> this manuscript was prepared with AI assistance. The scientific content — the
> entities depicted, the relationships between them, and the claim the figure
> makes — was specified by the authors and derives from the results reported in
> this manuscript. AI assistance was used for composition, layout, and the
> accessibility audit of the figure. No experimental data, image, or mechanism
> was generated. The authors have reviewed the final figure and take full
> responsibility for its scientific accuracy.

Two things to check before the author submits it:

- The claim about what was generated must be **true for this figure**. If a
  step was drawn that the author did not specify, the second sentence is false
  and the declaration must be amended.
- If the policy prohibits generated imagery and only the specification was
  delivered, no declaration is owed — the figure is the author's own work.

## When the policy prohibits generated imagery

Deliver a specification, not a figure. It should contain everything needed to
draw the figure and nothing that constitutes the drawing:

- The claim, in one sentence
- The figure type and reading path
- The entity list, with the label text for each
- The relationships, each marked as established or proposed
- The palette, with role assignments and the audit output
- Layout notes — grouping, opacity of context, panel structure

An author or a designer can draw from that, and the result is unambiguously
theirs.
